Read-only access
----------------
/dev/diag exists with CONFIG_DIAG_CHAR=y. The modem's registration table
contains EFS subsystem 19. A static temporary helper opens /dev/diag and
routes MODEM diagnostic traffic to a userspace memory session using the
exact kernel's packed 13-byte ioctl argument (mode=2, peripheral_mask=2,
pd_mask=0, mode_param=0). DIAG_CON_MPSS is 0x0002, distinct from the APSS
mask 0x0001; initial APSS-only attempts timed out before this was corrected.
Each client's close restores diagnostic routing; final status confirms
Logging Mode=0, MD session mode=0 and mask=0, matching initial state.

The helper accepts only version, EFS HELLO, OPEN with flags=0/permissions=0,
CLOSE, READ (at most 1024 bytes), OPENDIR, READDIR, CLOSEDIR and STAT.
Write, create, delete, rename, NV-write and arbitrary DIAG commands are
rejected. Requests use HDLC CRC framing; client responses require a valid
CRC and matching subsystem/opcode. Directory/file/total-size limits and
request deadlines bound collection. The helper has a 30-minute watchdog
and was compiled with -Wall -Wextra -Werror. Native guard tests passed
for disallowed opcodes, every tested nonzero open flag, oversized reads,
oversized paths and wrong subsystem. No log-stream enable commands,
assistance injection or EFS content writes were sent.

Seven existing records changed in 255 bytes altogether, and two new
128-byte records appeared. Nine affected record payloads total 896 bytes.
No files were removed. The fixed snapshot showed only AlmFile19 changed;
the remaining updates were visible by the post-release snapshot. They
could have occurred during the remainder of the active session, grace or
shutdown; the endpoint comparison does not isolate the exact trigger.

Names indicate GPS almanac and GLONASS ephemeris cache records. Almanacs
provide coarse satellite orbit/status information for acquisition, while
ephemerides support accurate satellite-position calculation. General
reference: https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/GPS_Navigation_Message
Binary record fields and file-index-to-satellite-ID mapping were not
decoded, so no individual orbital parameter or PRN/slot claim is made.

Captured files that did not change include the four GPS/GLO/GAL/QZSS X3
assistance files, GPS-specific NV settings, SUPL certificate files,
/GNSS/SAML calibration files and /GNSS/ME receiver-estimate files.
The rest of modem EFS was not compared. These observations do not rule
out changes to filesystem metadata or other uncollected namespaces.

Safety and implications
-----------------------
All six protected partition physical write counters stayed zero in the
initial/final status and every timed sample: modemst1, modemst2, fsc,
fsg_a, fsg_b, persist. All four live backing-partition SHA-256 hashes
matched those recorded after the preceding RAM export. GPS finished OFF
with no leases, CID, grace, failures or manager-reported leaked clients.
Installed binaries/boot images were unchanged; the helper is only in /run.

This establishes real, small GNSS-file updates behind the whole-image
RMTFS shadow rewrites. It gives a concrete candidate namespace for future
selective persistence, rather than assuming every NV change is GPS state.
It does not establish an encrypted-image decoder, a safe restore format,
cache validity rules, or a cross-boot TTFF improvement. Preserving these
files may help acquisition; that still requires restore design and a
controlled before/after test. Original partitions remain read-only.
