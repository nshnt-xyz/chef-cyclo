1. Finding (live, installed pair 1bb8a700 + a2ca8337, up 13 h 37 min, panel off,
   USB attached)
- Load average a steady 5.0. Over 10 s: user 304 + sys 707 of 8000 jiffies busy
  (8 CPUs, HZ 100), about 12.6 % of the machine, i.e. one core.
- top: /usr/bin/fblog in state R at 12 % of the machine (one core); every other
  process 0 to 1 %. /proc/<fblog>/stat: utime + stime about 48934 s of CPU in
  about 49000 s of uptime; voluntary context switches 9, involuntary 546159.
  fds: /dev/kmsg, /run/fb0.lock, /dev/fb0, no inotify fd (so it was in the main
  loop, paused). /proc/<pid>/syscall read "running" 8 of 8 times.
- Cause in tools/fblog/fblog.c next_timeout_ms(): while paused (a foreground
  client holds the exclusive screen lock) tick() returns at the failed shared
  lock try and never commits, so the last commit time never advances, the time
  since the last commit passes the 1000 ms heartbeat and the timeout is 0
  forever: poll(kmsg, 0) plus flock(LOCK_SH|LOCK_NB) in a tight loop. The cap
  `if (paused && t > FB_LOCK_RETRY_MS)` only lowers a timeout, it never raises
  0. The old unit test even expected 100 for next_timeout_ms(true, 0, true, 0).
  It began when chef-settings started holding the screen from boot (system UI
  slice 1, 2026-10-11).
- SIGSTOP proof (USB attached, 25 x 1 Hz means of battery current_now, 3 s
  settle, 3 cycles, negative = charging): fblog running -99.6, -55.0, -126.5 mA;
  fblog stopped -190.2, -187.9, -190.9 mA. Stopping it raised the charge
  current by 91, 133 and 64 mA (mean 96 mA). This explains the 2026-10-16
  Phase A base of about 198 mA against about 95 mA on 2026-10-04.

6. Conclusions (idle_coord)
C1. The regression is fixed. Base idle is 100.4 mA against about 198 mA on
    2026-10-16 and about 95 mA on 2026-10-04. The whole 98 mA came from fblog
    spinning a core while chef-settings held the screen (SIGSTOP check 96 mA).
    The remaining 5 mA against 2026-10-04 is not explained by any resident; it is
    within day-to-day differences (other image, battery level and temperature,
    a paired BLE sensor). Not chased.
C2. No resident costs anything worth acting on: stopping gps-manager (DISABLED),
    sensors-up, chef-settings (dark) or blesensd moves the mean by 0.6 to 1.7 mA,
    1 to 2 times the base-block spread; consistent in sign on both sides for all but
    UI (-1.6, +0.3); GPS and SENS lower it, BLE raises it slightly. Each is at
    most about 2 mA.
C3. Bluetooth powered on (adapter up, page scan, one bonded BLE sensor waiting)
    costs about 2 mA (BTOFF -1.9 mA). It is the only condition that changes the
    shape of the current: per-sample sd falls from 14..19 mA to 2.3 mA with the
    adapter off, so the 1 Hz spikes are the controller's scan windows, cheap on
    average.
C4. Stopping the BT stack (btattach/bt-up and bluetoothd) while the adapter was
    powered raises the draw by 3.7 mA (both neighbours +3.3/+4.1): with no host
    stack the QCA controller stays awake. Powering the adapter off through BlueZ
    (the settings switch) is the right way to save BT power; killing the stack
    is not. Nothing in the product does the latter, so no change.
C5. ALL (+2.3 mA) matches C4: the BTSTACK rise outweighs the small savings of
    the rest (sum of singles +0.5, difference 1.8 mA, about 2 base sds). No
    interaction worth a follow-up.
C6. The idle floor of about 100 mA with the screen off is now the platform
    itself, not our daemons (CPU busy 0.23 to 0.36 % in every block). The next
    lever is below userspace (no system suspend today; CPU idle and the
    remaining always-on rails): a separate investigation.
